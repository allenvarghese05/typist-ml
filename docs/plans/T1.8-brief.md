## 2. Why it matters

The baseline is the first phase every participant does (3.2: "Baseline | Day 0 and day 1 | Two
sessions of fixed test text (form A) covering the ~150 most common English letter bigrams"). The
whole experiment is built on it:

- **Choosing the weak bigrams (3.4)** requires candidates with "at least 20 first-attempt
  observations in the two baseline sessions". If a bigram does not occur often enough in form A, it
  can never become a candidate, so it can never be assigned to an arm (D01/D04). The top ~150
  bigrams are exactly the candidate pool (3.4 step 1: "letter-letter bigrams from the ~150 most
  common in English").
- **The corpus** (word list and, per section 7, word index + n-gram model in `data/`) is the shared
  source for the heuristic generator (13.3, T2.4), the Markov generator (13.4, T3.1), the control
  generator (13.2) and the real-word ratio gate (13.1, D16). If the corpus is wrong or unstable,
  every targeted arm and the fairness gate are wrong.
- Form A is **frozen once used** (3.2: baseline "fixed test text (form A)"; the pilot runbook: "Do
  not change code that affects telemetry, generation or text composition during the pilot"). A
  composition bug discovered after baseline sessions begin cannot be fixed without redoing the
  baseline (3.2 rule). The retest composer (form B, T2.7) is a parallel text that must reuse this
  task's machinery and "different words from form A where possible".

What breaks if it is wrong: too few occurrences → a bigram drops out of the candidate pool →
assignment runs on fewer than 16 bigrams (3.4 step 4: "If fewer than 16 qualify, take 12 ... and
log it") → fewer per set → noisier outcomes (18: "Few errors per bigram"). A corpus that is not
deterministic breaks the "recompute gives identical stats" / determinism promises (13.1: "Same spec
and seed gives the same text").

## 7. Edge cases (concrete)

1. **"15" (acceptance) vs "20 first-attempt observations" (3.4 step 1).** Acceptance asks for "15 or
   more occurrences" across the two baseline texts, but candidate eligibility needs "at least 20
   first-attempt observations in the two baseline sessions." Reconciliation: 3.2 says there are
   **two baseline sessions**, each typing form A. If form A gives a bigram 15 occurrences, two
   sessions give ~30 first-attempt observations (minus mistypes/backspaces that break the j-1 clean
   context). Example: bigram `th` appears 16 times in form A → 32 observations across two sessions →
   above 20. But "15 occurrences" is per *the two texts combined* vs per *each* text is ambiguous
   (open question 5). A bigram with exactly 15 occurrences and some mis-attributed context could
   fall below 20 observations.
2. **"the top ~150 bigrams" is undefined.** There is no committed list of the 150 most common
   English letter-letter bigrams. It must be derived (from `wordfreq`? from a standard corpus
   frequency table?). The derivation method changes which bigrams must be covered. Example: whether
   `qu` or `wh` is in the top 150 depends on the source. (Open question 6.)
3. **Rare bigrams are hard to pack.** Some of the top-150 bigrams are uncommon enough that reaching
   15 occurrences inside a ~natural-looking lowercase English text of bounded length is hard.
   Example: `ck`, `wk`, `ph` each need 15 hits without blowing up text length or repeating words
   beyond readability. The composer must balance coverage against text length and word repetition.
4. **Space-crossing pairs must be excluded.** Example: text `"to go"` — the pairs are `to`, `go`
   only; `o␣g` and `␣g` are not bigrams. A naive sliding window over the full string would wrongly
   count them. `bigram_counts` for `"to go"` must be `{"to": 1, "go": 1}`.
5. **Two texts, two `part` values, both shared.** Both rows have `participant_id=None`, so the
   partial unique index `uq_text_passages_shared_kind_form_part` on `(kind, form, part)` requires
   distinct `part` values (e.g. 0 and 1). Re-running the composer against a DB that already holds
   form A rows would hit the unique index. Example: a second `typist corpus build` run → insert
   conflict unless the command is idempotent or clears prior form A rows. (Open question 7.)
6. **Determinism across `wordfreq` versions.** If `wordfreq` is unpinned or upgraded, the word list
   and derived bigram frequencies change, so the "same seed, same text" guarantee silently breaks
   across environments. Must be pinned in the lockfile (13.1; 18 "Library changes mid-project").
7. **`JSONText` cannot store a JSON null.** `bigram_counts` must be a `dict[str, int]`; a bigram
   with zero count should be omitted (as in the seed fixture), never stored as `null`.
8. **Repetition in baseline text.** 3.4 and the fairness rules disallow pathological repetition in
   *drills*; baseline text has no stated repetition rule, but packing rare bigrams by repeating one
   word (e.g. `"back back back ..."`) would make a degenerate test. No design rule bounds this for
   form A (open question 8).
9. **Corpus-vs-composer coupling.** The "~150 most common bigrams" list is both what the corpus
   build must compute and what the composer must cover. If the two use different sources, the
   composer may "cover" bigrams that are not actually the top 150. (Open question 6.)
10. **Retest reuse (T2.7, form B).** T2.7 needs "different words from form A where possible". If the
    composer hard-codes form A words, T2.7 cannot avoid them. The composer should be parameterised
    by form/word-exclusion, but T2.7 is out of scope here — note the coupling.
11. **Session wiring does not exist.** There is no API/planner to create a baseline session's blocks
    from these passages yet (T1.6 answers 8/9; that is T2.6/T1.9). T1.8's output is just the
    `text_passages` rows; verifying the acceptance via actual typed sessions is not possible yet, so
    the acceptance must be checked against `bigram_counts` / the text directly.

## 8. Risks

1. **Under-covering the top ~150 bigrams.** The most likely failure: rare bigrams fall short of 15
   occurrences, so the acceptance test fails or passes only for ~140 bigrams. This cascades into
   fewer assignment candidates (3.4 step 4) and noisier results.
2. **Wrong "top 150" source.** Choosing a different bigram-frequency source than the one the rest of
   the pipeline expects produces a mismatched candidate pool that nobody notices until assignment.
3. **Non-determinism.** Unseeded sampling or an unpinned `wordfreq` breaks the "same seed, same
   text" rule and makes form A irreproducible; worst after the baseline is frozen.
4. **Dependency/lockfile churn under the sandbox.** Adding `wordfreq` (and possibly numpy) requires
   a lockfile update; the sandbox blocks network and some owner-run steps, so `uv sync --locked`
   may need the owner to regenerate the lock first.
5. **Counting space-crossing pairs or miscounting** → `bigram_counts` disagrees with attribution's
   definition (§4.3), corrupting the coverage check the column exists for.
6. **Idempotency.** A composer that is not safe to re-run collides with the partial unique index or
   produces duplicate/garbage `text_passages` rows in `data/typist.db`.
7. **Scope creep.** `corpus build` per 14.1 includes "word index and n-gram model" (used by T2.4 /
   T3.1). Building more than the baseline composer needs risks pulling T2/T3 work into T1.8;
   building too little risks `typist corpus build` not matching its 14.1 contract.

### Owner decision on session length (2026-10-19, owner)

Option **A**: each baseline session types **one** part of form A (day 0 → part 0, day 1 → part 1).
Parts are ~2,700 chars each (≈10–12 minutes per session, matching 3.2). The T1.8 acceptance is
met literally (≥15 occurrences per tracked bigram across the two texts combined). Consequence:
a bigram then has ≈15 first-attempt observations over the two sessions, below 3.4's "at least
20"; that threshold must be revisited (lowered to match `baseline_min_occurrences`, or the
candidate pool accepted as smaller) by the assignment task (T2.2) and recorded in an ADR. This
supersedes owner answer 5's "each session types all of form A" reading and the architect's
provisional 5,400-char default. `baseline_part_chars` default: the smallest value that gives
every tracked bigram ≥ 15 (combined) with a margin of at least +3 under the plan's composer,
targeting ~2,700; the architect records the chosen value.
